About the game

Eclipsis: The Shattered Expanse is an open-world survival sim in the Kenshi lane. Nineteen factions hold their own cities, feed their own people and go to war without asking you. Every fourteenth day at noon the sun goes black. You start as nobody in particular, and the world keeps a record of what you did to it. The world is drawn so it reads at a glance: people, buildings and objects you can tell apart from across the square.

The sun goes out on a schedule

Nothing exploded here. The old world was switched off. The civilisation everyone calls the Architects did not fall — they stopped answering, and left their roads running true, their towers humming on clear nights and their machines still executing the last instruction they were given. Every fourteenth day at noon the sun goes black for a quarter of a day. The Cog Preachers call it their holiest hour. Something in the Machine Nexus wakes up for it.

A world that does not wait

Enemy factions clash on sight whether or not you are on the map. Caravans haul real cargo to towns that really need it, and towns eat, so a bad season on the breadbasket is a famine three regions away and a price you can see move. Cities pay their guards out of a treasury, and a bankrupt one loses them. Wars start over something, cost something, and end. Leave the game running and it will still have news for you at dawn.

What you actually do

Farm the ground you hold, smoke the catch before it spoils, and haggle the surplus into a town whose prices you can watch move. Build the bench, make the first thing at it, and climb from stone toward the machines the Architects left running. Run contraband past a gate frisk and fence it in a back room. Take a bounty, or become one. Bury the companions you lose under a cut headstone with your name on it, or dig up somebody else’s dead and find out that grave goods are the one thing no lawful counter in the Expanse will touch.

Losing is a place the game continues from

A fight has stances, blocks, dodges and charged heavies, and it can go badly in ways that are not a reload. Limbs are lost and replaced with prosthetics. Go down in the wrong territory and you wake in a cell, in a work gang, in a slaver's camp, or being carried off by the Hollowed to whoever they answer to — and getting out is the next thing you do.

Every system is wired to every other one

There is no separate quest layer sitting on top of a diorama. A cull contract on the bar's work board exists because a den three days away has been growing while nobody burned it. A rumour in that bar was minted from something that actually happened to somebody. The Codex fills in as you live, the History tab writes the world's own chronicle day by day, and a marker stands on the ground where the thing it names took place.

Drawn by code

The world is drawn at runtime by the code that runs it. That is why a town can be a hexagon of brick one seed and an octagon the next, why an L-shaped building is solid where the wing is and walkable in the notch, and why the whole world can be re-dressed by a mod. The grade is deliberately faded: the only vivid things in the Expanse are blood, fire, an alert, the arcane, and the corona.

Built to be rebuilt

Factions, creatures, recipes, quests, items, reactions and most of the rules are plain data files, and the base game is loaded through the same path a mod is. A mod folder merges over them, an executable verifier checks it is honest, and a complete total conversion ships as a worked example. If you want to make a different game with this one, that is the design rather than an accident.

Features

  • Nineteen factions with their own cities, territories, economies, named leaders, succession, wars and long memories.
  • One continuous 75,000-unit map: six biomes, sixteen named lands, and walled towns you get into through the gate like everyone else.
  • A nineteen-species ecology with predators, scavengers, dens that escalate into broodmother nests, seasonal migrations and three apex beasts waiting at fixed lairs.
  • Seven ages of technology across eleven disciplines, climbed by hand — or studied out of books and charts you buy off an archive shelf or take off a body.
  • Crime as a system: contraband, gate frisks, witnesses, bounty hunters, four kinds of custody, and fences who buy what no shop will.
  • Companions who carry moods, remember what you did, and want paying — recruit, equip, command and take direct control of any of them.
  • Optional local-LLM NPC dialogue with per-character memory, running entirely on your machine. No account, no internet, and written lines when it is off.

New in Playtest 14f12 — nobody walks through a wall: Playtest 14f11 put a face on the people; this one dresses them for it, stops them walking through walls, and opens the gaol from every door. Dave saw NPCs cross town walls, and it is fixed. The tester measured it first: thirteen bodies through a curtain wall in five game minutes. The movement code was cleared by count — no path ever walked a clear body into a wall. Bodies were being placed inside walls, and the anti-trap escape then walked them out through the far side. Every placement now resolves to clear ground and the escape walks toward the nearest way out, so patrols and travellers take the gate; the probe over 1500 ticks reads zero on every count. Not fixed here: three shopkeepers stand in a wall brick at world build because their building was raised overlapping the ring — they never move and never cross. Hoods belong to who wears them. The road hood is on the armorer’s shelf for 12 credits, and buying one puts your own face under it. Scouts, outriders, the Eclipse Choir, the Penumbra and the road bandits wear hoods while their champions and heavies keep the steel — about one body in five, with the two cult capitals hooded to a man on purpose. Nineteen leaders have nineteen heads: bare-faced where you are meant to know them, hooded where you are not, crowned visors where the office is the point. The gaol opens from every door. Captured on the spot, delivered by a slaver, or starting in a cell all land you in a dressed gaol — barred cells, a rack under a lamp, the bench, two wardens, another prisoner. The “bare gaol” reported in 14f10 was a test staging its own camera and does not reproduce in play. There is a new start, Sentenced: a Corona Wardens cell, an eight-day term, a ransom of 32 credits, eighteen coins and one bandage. The custody card gets out of your way on any movement or answer key, and Q brings it back. The sign tells you how to say it: Ashen Camp Gaol (“jayl”). Underneath, a body with no name was getting the same word every day as every other nameless body; the word an NPC calls you now comes off the same identity the crowd already uses for its face. Known and not fixed here: on the Sentenced start the Wardens levy their territory toll on you in their own cell; the floor row reads “Ground Floor” inside a cell; a slave collar files into the helm slot; long speaker names clip on the talk card. Your playtest 14 through 14f11 saves load; nothing here changes the shape of a save. Coming next: shadows that lengthen with the sun.

Windows

  1. Unzip the archive somewhere you can write to — your Desktop or Documents is fine. Do not run it from inside the zip.
  2. Run eclipsis.exe.
  3. Windows SmartScreen will warn you that it does not recognise the app. This build is unsigned pre-release software, so that warning is expected. Choose More info, then Run anyway.
  4. First launch takes a few seconds longer than the rest while the world is generated.

macOS

  1. Unzip the archive and put Eclipsis.app somewhere you can write to.
  2. If you downloaded it with a browser, the first launch is blocked: macOS says “Apple could not verify ‘Eclipsis’ is free of malware that may harm your Mac”, and that box has no open option. That is expected — the build is ad-hoc signed but not notarised. Click Done, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway next to Eclipsis, and confirm with Touch ID or your password. After that it is an ordinary double-click, forever.
  3. On macOS 15 and later that is the only route in the interface — the old right-click → Open shortcut no longer works. If you did not use a browser — curl, AirDrop, a USB stick, a copy from another Mac — there is no quarantine flag and it simply opens. Prefer Terminal? xattr -cr /path/to/Eclipsis.app does the same job as the settings step.
  4. One download runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel.
  5. First launch takes a few seconds longer than the rest while the world is generated.

Linux

  1. Extract the tarball to a folder of your choice.
  2. Make the binary executable: chmod +x eclipsis.x86_64 — or, in your file manager, Properties → Permissions → allow executing as a program.
  3. Launch it from the terminal or by double-clicking it in the file manager.
  4. First launch takes a few seconds longer than the rest while the world is generated.

Send the log with any bug report. The build has no console window, so its log file is the only window into what happened. It is written every run, in the game's user-data folder: on Windows %APPDATA%\Eclipsis\logs\godot.log; on macOS ~/Library/Application Support/Eclipsis/logs/godot.log; on Linux ~/.local/share/Eclipsis/logs/godot.log. (Builds before playtest 12 wrote to a folder under app_userdata; the first launch of this one copies those saves across by itself and leaves the old folder alone.) Attach that file, say roughly what you were doing, and that is a complete report.

This is a pre-alpha playtest

That is a real description rather than a modest one. Expect bugs, including ones that lose you a session. Audio is new and thin — fourteen effects, volume sliders, and a folder you can drop your own music into, but no original soundtrack. The art is drawn by code and is still being worked on; several systems look plainer than they will. Saves are not guaranteed to survive the next build. Balance is provisional everywhere, and some corners of the map are thinner than others.

A run-based mode — pick a seed, play until the waste takes you, and the next wanderer inherits what the last one learned — is in design and is not in this build. Nothing on this page describes it as present.

This page is unlisted: it is not indexed, not announced, and not linked from anywhere public. Please do not repost the link or the build.

If you play it, tell me what happened — what confused you, what you tried that the game did not expect, and what made you stop. That is the whole reason this build exists.
