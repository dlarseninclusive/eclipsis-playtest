What changed

Each of these was found by playing, measured across hundreds of generated worlds, and verified afterwards on the same worlds.

Long hauls arrive

#10

A click across the continent used to give up. Measured over ninety-nine worlds: three arrivals before, eighty-two per cent arriving or all but arriving after.

The way down

#8

A fitting on the roof deck sat fifty-two pixels from the stair mouth, inside the fifty-four-pixel reach of the prop scan that runs first — so E answered with the furniture and you were stranded upstairs. Descending went from twenty-eight per cent to a hundred.

The towns that ate themselves

#7

Far from the map's origin, float32 lost enough precision that a body's own torso failed to triangulate and the errors killed the process. Machine Nexus was the first town over the line; it is not any more.

The details that went missing

#9

The same distance quietly stopped the small things being drawn at all — weathervanes and gables on the rooftops, the tails that point a speech bubble at whoever is speaking, the icons on dropped loot. Far towns wear them again.

Roads, and what they cost

new

Dave's bargain: the road is the safe way and the taxed one. Seventy-nine per cent of a long route now lies on a road, predators hunt at less than half their range near one, and the ambushers who work the highways trigger at twice the distance.

Where to look

Worth your attention this round

  • Walk somewhere far. Open the map with M and click across the continent. It should get there — and it should take the road.
  • Do the roads feel like a bargain? Fewer animals, more people who want your cargo. That trade is new, and the numbers behind it are guesses until somebody plays them.
  • Go upstairs, then come back down. Both directions, in as many buildings as you can stand.
  • Visit somewhere remote. The far towns used to come apart. They should not now.
  • Carry a gun. Firearms were drawing as strung bows and firing wooden arrows. They look right now; they do not yet sound right.

Known, and not worth reporting

Already on the list

  • The Ollama prompt on first launch. Dialogue offers to install a local model before you reach the menu. Skip is fine — you get hand-written faction lines instead of generated ones.
  • Guns sound like bowstrings. The art is fixed; the audio is not. Parked.
  • Short days, dark nights. A full cycle runs about two minutes and deep night sits low on purpose. Cycle your carried light — match, lighter, torch, lamp.
  • The window says “Godot Spike.” Cosmetic; it is the project’s working name.
