give hands you a real weapon

A way to target

A market ledger

  • Every price you have seen, by city, on a new MARKET tab in the Information panel. Cities you have not traded in say “no prices seen”.

Playtest 14f12 — nobody walks through a wall

Released 2026-09-08

A twelfth follow-up to playtest 14. Playtest 14f11 put a face on the people; this one dresses them for it, stops them walking through walls, and opens the gaol from every door.

Nobody walks through a wall

  • Dave saw NPCs cross town walls. It is fixed. The tester measured it first: thirteen bodies walked clean through a curtain wall in five game minutes, four Automaton sentries five times each. The movement code was cleared by count: no path ever walked a clear body into a wall. Bodies were being placed inside walls — squads posted on the town ring, travellers marching to a city centre inside the ring, a beast rounded half a pixel into a brick — and the anti-trap escape then walked them out through the far side.
  • Every placement now resolves to clear ground, and the escape walks toward the nearest way out instead of wherever the body was already going. Patrols and travellers take the gate. The tester’s probe over 1500 ticks reads zero on every count.
  • Not fixed here: three shopkeepers stand in a wall brick at world build because their building was raised overlapping the ring. They never move and never cross.

Hooded, by who they are

  • The road hood is on the armorer’s shelf for 12 credits. Buy one and your own face goes under it.
  • Scouts, outriders, the Eclipse Choir, the Penumbra and the road bandits wear hoods. Their champions and heavies keep the steel. About one body in five is hooded; the two cult capitals are hooded to a man, on purpose.
  • Nineteen leaders, nineteen heads. Some are bare-faced because you are meant to know them, some hooded because you are not, some behind a crowned visor because the office is the point, two in an open military helm. Dave rules each one; the table is in the data.

The gaol, from every door

  • Captured on the spot, delivered by a slaver, or starting your game in a cell all land you in a dressed gaol: barred cells, a rack under a lamp, the bench, two wardens, another prisoner, the barred gate. The “bare gaol” reported in 14f10 turned out to be a test staging its own camera; it does not reproduce in play.
  • A new start, “Sentenced”: you begin in a Corona Wardens cell with an eight-day term, a ransom of 32 credits, eighteen coins and one bandage.
  • The custody card gets out of your way. Any movement or answer key dismisses it; Q brings it back.
  • The sign tells you how to say it: Ashen Camp Gaol (“jayl”). On the sign and the custody header, nowhere else.

The written voice, fixed underneath

  • A body with no name was getting the same word every day as every other nameless body. The word an NPC calls you now comes off the same identity the crowd already uses for its face, so it is stable per body and varies across bodies. Every spawned NPC has a name; this only mattered for hand-built ones.

Also

  • The cinematic key gained a headless check, so the suite no longer fails the family in a headless run.
  • Store screenshots 01–04 and 08 are re-cut: hooded scouts in Rust Haven, road travellers in the storm, hooded bodies in two interiors.

Known, and not fixed here

  • On the Sentenced start the Wardens levy their territory toll on you in their own cell (18 → 3 credits in seconds).
  • The floor row reads “Ground Floor” inside a cell.
  • A slave collar files into the helm slot on the body. Pre-existing.
  • Long speaker names clip on the name bar of the talk card.
  • Round-building collision is still rectangular.
  • The top speed rung stays at x4, by decision.

The journal is still logs/journal.jsonl in the game’s user folder, with F12 to mark a moment and save a frame — a body inside a wall is exactly the sort of thing to mark, and the seed from --seed handed over with it lets us stand where you stood.

Playtest 14f11 — a face under the hood

Released 2026-09-08

An eleventh follow-up to playtest 14. Playtest 14f10 put the things on the floor; this one puts a face on the people, fixes the line the local model was eating, and gives the fallback voice three lines per stance instead of two.

A face under the hood

  • What you wear on your head decides your face. Bare-headed and you are shown; a hood or a closed visor and you are hooded. Both are drawn, both are real readings; there is no third state.
  • The talk card carries a portrait above the speech box, and the armour on it reads as a material — cloth, hide, scrap, plate — not a colour.
  • Automatons, servitors and the Hollowed keep their own faces: the optic band, the scan slit, the two points of light. Nobody drew a human face on them.
  • Painted portraits can be dropped in later. The route is in; no paintings ship yet.

The lines the model was eating

  • A quotation mark was stopping the local model at character zero. Small models open a spoken line with one, and the game read that as an empty reply and showed the canned line — which looked exactly like Ollama being off. Fixed, and measured: on the two small Gemma models a quarter of replies came back empty before, and none do now, at the same latency. If you run Ollama, you will hear the model far more often.
  • A model that keeps returning nothing is set aside for the session and the next one on the list is tried, then the written line.

The written voice

  • Three lines per faction per stance, a hundred and seventy-one in all, so a faction you meet twice in a minute does not repeat itself.
  • Lines carry your name, the land you stand in, and what that faction calls someone like you — a hostile Rust Walker and a friendly one do not use the same word for you.

For the trailer

  • The HUD can be hidden. F10 takes the whole interface off and puts it back; hud off and hud on in the console do the same; --cinematic on the command line starts that way. It is never saved. Hiding it is a view preference, not a save field: a save reloaded inside the same session stays as you left it, and a fresh launch without the flag shows the HUD.

Also

  • Four new store screenshots on the page.

Known, and not fixed here

  • A gaol cell rendered without its dressing was reported in 14f10. It has not been reproduced in play; it was reproduced only by calling the gaol path directly from a test. The captive-start and gaol-arm pass is the next cut regardless.
  • The road hood is in the data but not for sale anywhere yet. In play the hooded reading comes off a leader’s crowned helm. Next cut it is on the armorer’s shelf, scouts and outriders wear one on the road, and each faction’s leader shows or hides their face by that faction’s own rule.
  • A slave collar files into the helm slot on the body. Pre-existing.
  • Long speaker names clip on the name bar of the talk card.
  • Round-building collision is still rectangular.
  • The top speed rung stays at x4, by decision.

Playtest 14f8 — every building is its own building

Released 2026-09-07

An eighth follow-up to playtest 14. Every building in a town used to be the same box with a different label on it. Now a bar looks like a bar, a bank looks like a bank, and you can read a street before you walk down it.

Every building is its own building

  • One design per building kind. A bar, a bank, a palace, a guild hall, a safehouse, a cache, a slaver camp, a gaol, a mine, three kinds of dwelling, and a gate leaf that reads differently open and closed — each one distinct on its wall material and tone, its roof form and tone, its height, its plan family (block, hall, ell, court, drum) and its window style.
  • Shops differ by trade. The weaponsmith is round; the armorer, the grocer, the general trader, the fence and the slave shop each have their own build. You can tell what a shop sells from across the square.
  • Houses keep the faction roof colour, deliberately. A street should still tell you whose town you are standing in.
  • Round buildings are drawn inscribed in their footprint. Their collision is still rectangular — that is a known gap, not a surprise.

The speed ladder

  • Time now runs at x1, x2 or x4 — it was 1, 3 and 8. Period cycles 1 → 2 → 4.
  • Full x8 is removed rather than promised. Playtest 14f7 made the pill honest about a speed the game could not deliver; this build takes the honest step of not offering it. The ladder tops at x4.
  • The pill still shows the multiple actually achieved, and now rounds down — TIME x4 → x2.8 ». If the world is running slower than you asked, the number never flatters it.

Reading the game

  • The title screen carries the painted wordmark, with the subtitle in the mark’s own white.
  • A caught blow that still hurt says how much got through. Blocking is not all-or-nothing, and the report no longer pretends it is.
  • Inside a labour camp or a gaol, the banner names the camp’s faction rather than the ground it happens to sit on.

Pick your Expanse

  • eclipsis.exe -- --seed <n> picks the world for a fresh New Game. The number can be hex or decimal. Continue is unaffected — a save keeps its own world, always.
  • seed <n> in the console does the same for the next world the running game builds.
  • If you find an Expanse worth showing someone, the seed is now a thing you can hand over.

Playtest 14f7 — the pill tells the truth

Released 2026-09-07

A seventh follow-up to playtest 14. The speed control has been lying to you: pick x8 and the world ran at roughly a third of it, while the pill kept saying x8. This build makes the pill tell the truth, and makes x8 a good deal better than it was.

The TIME pill

  • The pill shows the speed the game actually achieves when it cannot keep up — TIME x8 → x1.5 — instead of claiming a speed it is not delivering. If the world is running slower than you asked, you can now see it, and by how much.
  • No game time is lost between frames. Time that the simulation could not consume is carried rather than dropped, so a day is a day whatever the frame rate did.
  • At x8 the picture updates about three times more often than before. This is a real improvement and it is not the whole fix: a full-speed x8 needs the simulation itself to get cheaper, and that is the next build.

Reading the alerts

  • Alert rows put their number first and wrap instead of cutting it off, so you get 30 labour (10 shifts) · Dust Reavers and Dust — sight 50% in full rather than a truncated line.
  • Custody reads in labour and shifts, the same units the wall counts in.

Small mercies

  • A drink takes what the body has room for, and says how much.

Under the hood

  • The frame-time watch in the journal writes one line a second instead of one per frame, so the journal stays readable and stays small.

Playtest 14f6 — every blow on the record

Released 2026-09-06

A sixth follow-up to playtest 14. Playtest 14f4 gave fights a voice, but a beating still happened in silence — and that turned out to be the worst case of all, because a beating is exactly when you most need to know what is happening to you.

Every blow on the record

  • Every blow you take is now in the combat report and the journal. Contact damage arrives as a trickle rather than as discrete hits, so a beating produced no lines at all. Blows are now gathered per attacker per second and reported with the whole of the damage, so the record matches what your health bar is doing.
  • Going down writes its own line. Losing consciousness is an event, and it now says so.
  • A blow your armour turned reads as absorbed. It used to report as “bleeding” for zero damage, which described nothing that happened.

Being held

  • The people who captured you stand down inside the camp for your term. They used to keep beating you where you fell, which made serving a sentence a matter of surviving your gaolers. They take up arms again only if you run.
  • The banner over a camp that granted you passage says passage, not hostile ground.
  • The gate of a camp you have served your sentence in says leave, not run.

Under the hood

  • The game frees the worlds it builds. Worlds were being created and never released; the test suite’s memory use drops from about 50 GB to 17 GB. You should not see this at all — which is the point — but a long session has less reason to grind.

Your playtest 14 through 14f5 saves load. Nothing here changes the shape of a save. The journal is still logs/journal.jsonl in the game’s user folder, with F12 to mark a moment and save a frame — and if you take a beating and the report does not match what your health did, that is the moment to press it.

Playtest 14f5 — what is still true

Released 2026-09-06

A fifth follow-up to playtest 14. The log tells you what happened; it is bad at telling you what is still true. A raid on your base, a debt coming due, the fact that you have not drunk in two days — all of that scrolled past once and was gone. This build fixes that.

The alert stack

Down the right edge of the HUD, a stack of standing conditions. An alert appears the moment its condition becomes true and vanishes the moment it stops — it is a state, not a message, so nothing important can scroll away while you are looking elsewhere. What can appear there:

  • A raid on your base, while it is coming.
  • Custody — that you are being held, and by whom.
  • Thirst or hunger past the first rung of the deprivation ladder.
  • A bounty on you with hunters out.
  • Broken gear.
  • A levy due, or in arrears.
  • Famine in a town you are friendly with.
  • The weather in force.
  • Passage held with a faction.

Karma

  • Your character card carries a karma line with the band you are in — reviled, grim, unremarked, decent, beloved — and the last three things that moved it, so the number has a history you can read rather than a value you have to trust.
  • Crossing into a new band says so.

The legibility sweep

Five things that used to change your state in silence now tell you:

  • Refusing a toll costs you standing, and now says how much.
  • A faction reacting to something you did says so, instead of the number quietly moving.
  • Wiping out a faction gives every one of its enemies +20 towards you — a real and large effect, now announced rather than discovered later.
  • Each new injury zone carries a penalty when you are knocked out, and the penalty is named.
  • Weather affects how far you can see, and says so when it does.

Playtest 14 — the forty-eight-minute day

Released 2026-09-04

The pacing build. Playtest 12 said time ran too fast and the bars emptied faster; playtest 13 said the fix was designed but not in the box. This is the box. A day is forty-eight minutes now, there is exactly one clock, and everything that was quietly counted in real seconds — hunger, sentences, interest, caravans — is counted in game time instead.

The clock

  • A day takes forty-eight minutes, up from two — Kenshi’s base day. The x1/x3/x8 speed control is unchanged, so x8 is a six-minute day when you want the world to get on with it.
  • There is only one clock. The whole world runs on it, so a “day” means the same thing to your thirst, a town’s food bill, a courier’s deadline, a crop, a war and a wage. An earlier draft gave the economy a second clock of its own; that was thrown away.
  • Travelling by road no longer skips the dawn. Fast travel across midnight used to leave wages unpaid, towns unfed and the day unwritten. The hours a road eats now go through the same clock the world does.

Staying alive

  • Thirst is twelve game hours, hunger twenty-four — drink about twice a day, eat about once — and because they are authored in game time they mean that at any length of day.
  • Going without has rungs, not a cliff. A second day without water slows you and halves your healing; a third stops healing altogether and blunts your damage and your skills. You read as Dehydrated, and you begin blacking out for a moment every couple of hours.
  • Heat can no longer kill you. The noon sun will take a parched body to its last hit point and leave it there. Thirst can wreck you; it does not finish you.
  • A meal fills the bar to exactly full, never past it — and a mouthful that merely lifts you off zero pauses the ladder rather than wiping the days you have spent on it.
  • Sprinting costs what it should. Holding shift drains both bars at three times the resting rate, so a full canteen is four game hours of running. It used to be charged per real second, which at the new day length emptied a water bar in about eighty seconds.

The ledger

  • A missed protection levy is a tally, not a loan. It sits flat against the shop it was levied on instead of compounding on a lender’s book. It can cost you standing down to unfriendly and no further — that floor is deliberate; −100 is what a massacre earns.
  • You can finally pay one. “Settle” on the Holdings tab, or venue arrears. The shipped game had no way to clear an arrears at all.
  • Criminal factions send hunters. Three missed collections in a row from a faction whose word is protection puts a price on you through the same bounty ledger everything else uses — and settling the bill takes back exactly that, not a murder you had already earned.
  • The levy note says what it cost you and how deep the hole is, instead of moving your standing in silence.
  • Loans compound at 1% a game day, down from 5%. At the old rate an inherited forty-corona note crossed the collection threshold on day nine and turned a whole faction hostile against a player who had done nothing.
  • Old runaway debts are wiped once. A save carrying a seven-figure entry — the record was 2,086,916 coronas against 1,135 of real missed shop bills — is zeroed on load and the world writes a chronicle line saying so. What you actually owed a counter stays owed, and your standing is not forgiven.

Being held

  • Sentences are game days. A town cell is two, a lord’s hold four, a work gang seven, a slaver’s pen fourteen — and your bounty adds days on top, so killing a faction leader is about three game months. They were real seconds before, which made custody a menu with a countdown on it.
  • Serving it is a real way out. Nothing shortens a term for a player who sits still, and nothing forces you to escape. The wall tells you which day the door opens on, and the world moves over your head while you wait.

Everything else

  • Standing has a voice. A start tells you who it made you and who will not have you; crossing into a new band says so once, and not once per point; and a kill line names what it cost.
  • Your own faction’s city is not “hostile ground”. The zone readout was ORing a faction’s default hostility into its warning, so an Automaton stood in the Automaton capital and was told he was in danger.
  • The Great Eclipse is rare again — every fourteenth day, which is what the title screen has always said. Holding its old real-time rhythm under the longer day would have meant the sun dying at every single noon, which is weather, not an omen — and a cadence that disagreed with the game’s own premise line was not worth the minutes it saved. About eleven hours of play at normal speed, eighty-four minutes at x8.
  • Caravans leave every twenty-four minutes, twice a game day, with more of them on the roads at once.
  • Autosave every five minutes as well as at dawn — one save per forty-eight-minute day would have been a data-loss regression dressed up as a clock change.
  • The macOS build is ad-hoc signed, so its first launch is a click in Privacy & Security rather than a Terminal command.

One honest note. The base game is meant to be hard, and this build makes it harder before it makes it kinder. Difficulty settings and quality-of-life options are a slab of their own and they are not here yet — so if this feels punishing, that is the shape of the thing right now and worth telling us about anyway.

Your playtest 13 saves still load. Custody was never written to a save, so nobody wakes up in a cell they were not in; the deprivation counters start at zero; and the credit ledger heals itself once, as above.

Playtest 8 produced fifteen pieces of feedback in three minutes of play. All fifteen are in this build — nothing else jumped the queue. The theme, if there is one: the game stops being silent about things it always knew.

Features added

  • Custody is a place, not a menu. Getting arrested walks you to a real gaol — cells, a work bench, a barred gate, turnkeys and other prisoners — and the world keeps running around the bars. Your sentence visibly ticks down; you can wait it out, work it off at the bench, or try the gate. Esc opens the menu like anywhere else, and everything the screen tells you now fits on it.
  • Arrest costs coin — but never your savings. The law takes the value of your bounty or half the purse in your pocket, whichever is more, capped so a bad arrest is survivable. What you deposited at a bank is out of their reach — which is the reason to use one.
  • Interiors you can find your way out of. The street door is now the most findable thing on its wall — warm light spilling across the boards, lit frames, chevrons pointing out — and a shut door looks like a door instead of a dark patch of wall. Stairs carry a clear cue: a warm filled arrow points up, a cool open arrow points down, and the treads brighten toward where each flight goes.
  • Night rooms are lit by their lights. A hearth or a lamp now genuinely pushes back the dark — real light, not a faint glow losing to the night — while unlit cellars and empty houses stay exactly as dark as they should be.
  • Fast bodies run. A sprinting player (hold Shift), a fleeing villager, a charging fighter — anyone moving hard now visibly runs: longer stride, pumping arms, a lean into the movement. Walkers still walk.
  • The console teaches its own arguments. tp ? lists every destination; tp kr* filters by prefix; the same pattern works on every command — faction names, item ids, recipes, scenarios — and multi-word names paste back whole.

Bug fixes

  • No more mystery teleports. The silent jump was your release from custody, which flung you into the street without a word. Being transported, fined, or released each tells you what happened, as it happens.
  • The vault bounty explains itself. If someone sees you crack a bank vault, the alarm goes up at once and the game names who saw you. If nobody did, you keep your head start — but the bank counts its own strongbox, and the game says so. (And the reliably empty bank hall turned out to be a staffing gap in world generation — the hall now tells you nobody is behind the counter; an actual teller arrives with the next world rebuild.)
  • E leaves the way it entered. Shop and bar screens close on the same key that opened them, and buying moved to its own key — Enter, same as every other confirm. The panel prints its keys along the bottom so you never have to guess. (The bar screen previously answered no key but Esc at all.)
  • The build banner is readable. It has its own reserved strip of screen now — the character info steps aside while build mode is on — and a permanent test makes it impossible for anything to ever draw over it again.
  • The time-speed control is findable. . cycles x1 / x3 / x8 and genuinely speeds up long walks — hunger and thirst scale honestly with it. It always worked; it was written down nowhere. The Controls tab now has a “keys you will not find by guessing” section, generated from your actual keybinds.
  • A bar has one name. The sign over the door, the plaque on the counter, and the header of the trade screen all say the same thing now. (The plaque was reading a label that was never the building’s name.)

Playtest 8 — room to live

Released 2026-08-23

The buildings were too small, and everyone knew it. This build rebuilds the world at a larger scale — bigger buildings, a bigger map to hold them, stairs that stay where you left them — and finally makes entering build mode a decision instead of an accident.

Features added

  • Bigger buildings, and a bigger world to match. Every building is 1.6× larger, plots and town walls scaled with them, and the map grew from 75,000 to 120,000 across so the towns keep their distance from one another. The smallest hall in the world is now a real room: a stairwell, a clear walkway past it, and space for a table and chairs. Travel speed and travel costs scaled together, so a journey between capitals takes the same in-world time and coin it always did.
  • One stairwell per building. The stairs used to sit at opposite ends of the hall depending on which way you were going — climb at the east wall, and find the way onward at the west one. Every building now has a single stair shaft, in the same corner on every floor, with the up and down flights side by side. Wherever you are in a building, the stairs are where they were on the last floor.
  • The way down is drawn. A ground floor over a cellar used to show nothing at all where the descending stair was — you walked across an unmarked patch of floor and fell into the basement. The downward stair is now drawn as a window into the room below, the same way the upward one has always shown the floor above.
  • Houses are furnished. Roughly twice as many buildings have furniture in them — tables, seats, storage — and tables are placed first, so a home reads as a home rather than a bare hall with one barrel in the middle.
  • Old saves carry forward. A save from any earlier playtest loads into the new world with everything — you, your buildings, your marks and discoveries — moved to its right place on the bigger map, and the game tells you it did so.

Bug fixes

  • Build mode no longer lays walls by accident. It entered with a wall already selected and its only on-screen indicator hidden behind the health bars — so a stray press of B followed by ordinary clicks quietly raised little structures. It now enters with nothing selected: a click places nothing until you pick a structure with the number keys, a banner across the top of the screen says exactly which state the mode is in, and pressing B twice by mistake is harmless. Right-click still reclaims anything you placed, at a full refund.
  • Furniture no longer blocks doorways and stairs. Tables and crates were placed without checking what was underneath, so they could sit on top of a stair mouth or a doorway — a good share of why you kept bumping into stairs. One placement rule now applies to everything: nothing is put down on a door, a stair, or another piece of furniture.
  • A duel does not reach through a wall. Two fighters squaring off could land blows with a wall standing between them. A duel now requires the two to actually share the space they are fighting in.
  • A failed build does not kick up dust. Placing a structure you could not afford played the same puff of dust as a successful build. The dust now means what it says.

Playtest 7 — turn it down, and look again

Released 2026-08-23

A short polish release: a proper volume control at last, and a handful of places where the view under an open roof was still telling you something that was not true.

Features added

  • Audio settings. Press Esc and there is an Audio block on the settings screen — master, effects and music, each on its own slider. Your choices are remembered between runs. Until now the only volume control was the one on your speakers.
  • Sound has a distance to it. Zoom out to take in the whole town and the world gets quieter; zoom in on one street and it comes back up. What you hear follows where the camera is standing, instead of playing at one flat volume however far away you were.

Bug fixes

  • The keeper is not standing on the roof. With a shop's roof shut, the shopkeeper behind the counter was being drawn on top of it — a person apparently up on the tiles of the building they are actually inside. They stay under their roof now, and appear when it lifts.
  • Weather stops at an open roof. A sandstorm, and the other weather veils, used to be drawn straight across a building whose roof you had lifted, so you looked down into a room with a dust storm blowing through it indoors. The veils are cut away over an interior you can see into.
  • The message blip is quieter, and knows when to be quiet. The tick that plays when a line lands in the log was too loud, and a busy moment could fire a dozen at once into a rattle. Turned down, and it no longer stacks on itself.
  • Stairs are drawn to size. They were rendered oversized and loose, spilling past the run they occupy. They are tighter now and sit where the stairwell actually is.
  • The top of a building is outdoors. Standing on the top deck — a flat roof, a parapet — you were still being treated as under cover. Weather, light and the rest of the outdoor world reach you up there now, because they should.

Playtest 6 — the world under the roofs, fixed

Released 2026-08-23

Playtest 5 took the roofs off, which was the right thing to do and also the thing that exposed everything under those roofs that had never had to work before. This is the pass that went back in after it.

Features added

  • The build knows its own name. The title screen tells you which build you are running, and once per launch — quietly, in the background — asks whether a newer one exists. If there is one you get a single line and a Get it button. If there is not, or you are offline, or anything goes wrong at all, nothing happens: no error, no dialog, no waiting.
  • The cut got its polish. The roof fades over a quarter-second instead of popping off in a frame. The cut edge is a sliced wall with real thickness. The floor says whose floor it is — a woven mat in front of a counter, worn boards in a taproom, a scorch where the work gets done. And rooms have depth: inner shadow where the sun says it should fall, the floor below drawn dimmer and drained of colour, and a stairwell drawn as an actual shaft rather than a painted square.

Bug fixes

  • The labour camp is escapable. There is a gate in the yard and E at it runs for it. The custody card had been telling you to run for a gate that did not exist, which quietly made the slaver start a life sentence with two guards on it. That start is survivable now, both ways out of it.
  • Freed slaves get their gear back. Working off the quota the honest way now un-collars you and hands back the kit they stripped. It used to leave you in rags with your real weapon and armour stranded somewhere nothing would ever return them — and a second collar while still in rags destroyed them for good.
  • Doors work. Walk up to one and E opens it, closes it, picks the lock or puts your shoulder through it. Roughly a third of the doors in the world are locked, and until now not one of those four verbs could reach any door at all — you would find a sealed wing of a house and simply have nothing to do about it. A door you bash open is now genuinely open: to walking, to line of sight, and to everyone routing through it, instead of drawing open while still being a wall.
  • The down-strike from a battlement lands. Standing on a town wall, every body in the street below was filtered out of your swing before it happened, so the parapet strike and bow fire from the wall never connected — not rarely, never. In the street the same mistake ran the other way and you could melee someone a storey up through their floorboards.
  • Nobody hits you through a ceiling. In a cellar, a pursuer standing over you was beating you through the floor — and a chase parks one directly overhead every time, so this was not an edge case. Nor can a body on one roof reach into the house next door, or step off its roof and cross the street in mid-air.
  • About two hundred other proximity checks — aggro, witnesses, capture, squad combat, turrets, looting, wells, the crime ledger — now ask whether you and the other body are actually in the same space, instead of assuming a building is a solid block nobody can be inside.
  • Old saves land somewhere real. A save holds your character and your world's history, not its terrain, so one taken on an upper floor or a roof used to restore you onto a floor that does not exist in the newly generated world: standing in mid-air, untouchable and unable to touch anything. Every restored body is now landed properly, and loading the same file three times no longer leaves three rings of invisible masonry behind it.

Playtest 5 — one world

Released 2026-08-22

Until now, walking into a building was a magic trick: the screen cut to black, you appeared in a little room that existed nowhere, and the world outside stopped mattering. That whole second world is gone. There is one world, and buildings are part of it.

Features added

  • Walk in and the roof comes off. No loading, no cut, no separate room. You step through a door, the roof fades away so you can see the floor you are standing on, and the street you just came from is still right there out the window, still going on without you.
  • The guard follows you in. Break a law and run into a house and whoever is chasing you comes through the door after you and up the stairs. Fights work indoors: swings connect, arrows fly, someone on a battlement can strike down at someone below.
  • A locked door actually stops them. Unlocked, a pursuer simply opens it. Locked, they cannot get through at all, and they stop instead of grinding against the wood. That is a genuine escape.
  • Shopkeepers are people standing at counters. The counter is a real object at a real spot on a real floor and the keeper is a body behind it — the same character who walks home in the evening, sleeps, and comes back in the morning.
  • Your squad walks in with you, through the door like everybody else, instead of being spirited to your side.
  • Every verb works under a roof. Claim, capture, read, pickpocket, dodge, squad orders and stances all work in a room, because a room is just part of the world now. Surveying and digging correctly refuse — you cannot assay the ore under somebody's floorboards.
  • Walls and battlements ride the same system. A town wall, its stairs and its parapet are floors in the same model a house's upper storey uses, so a lookout on the wall and the gun beside them are two separate things your hand can reach for — and it picks the one you meant.
  • The labour camp is a real place — a walled compound on clear ground with its own yard and its own captors living in it, rather than a room you are teleported into.
  • You can look down the stairwell. On an upper floor the way down is cut open as a window, with the floor below drawn into it, dimmer, receding for each level under that.
  • One HUD tells you which building has you, whose it is, and which floor you are on.

Bug fixes

  • Buildings no longer stand inside each other. Towns had been quietly putting houses on top of their own neighbours for a long time — invisible while both were solid blocks, and immediately obvious once you could walk in and find yourself in the wrong house's hall.
  • Keyboard zoom is back. + and -, and the keypad pair, zoom exactly as the mouse wheel does. They had been deleted along with the old interior room, behind a comment claiming otherwise.
  • Hold-right-mouse-to-walk works everywhere, because there is no longer a second movement path that did not have it.
  • The keeper can leave the counter. Evening routines could get stuck behind a shopkeeper who would not stand down from a post.
  • Click-to-move into a room routes to the door instead of beelining into a wall, and a companion you are driving accepts that order outdoors too.

Playtest 4 — the cost of doing business

Released 2026-08-22

Playtest 3 let you own a shop. Playtest 4 sends you the bill.

Features added

  • Somebody always owes somebody. A venue you own carries a levy — rent to a landlord, tax to the faction that holds the ground, or protection to whoever really runs that street. It comes due whether or not you had a good week, and missing it makes the people you owe stop being polite about it.
  • Set your own markup. The price you post is a dial rather than a number handed to you. Charge what you like, but the town is paying attention: a famished town will swallow a cruel margin because it has no choice, a comfortable one will simply walk out.
  • Hire a smuggler. Stock does not walk to your door. Put a smuggler on retainer and they run your supply line — a real route, over real ground, with real things that can go wrong on it.
  • Run a back room. A second key turns your honest storefront into a speakeasy after hours. Contraband carries a premium, the racket takes its cut, and the trade you cannot do at the counter happens behind it.
  • Bribe somebody. Gate guards, coffle escorts, the tax assessor — every one of them can be bought and none of them is guaranteed. The offer is a roll against your charisma, your standing with their faction, and how incorruptible that faction is to begin with. Some people take the coin. Some people remember that you offered.

Bug fixes

  • The map no longer lies to a prisoner. Ordering travel while in custody used to be accepted in silence — the game said "Traveling to…" and you stood exactly where you were. Every way of giving a travel order now refuses out loud and says why. You can still open the map and look; looking is not going.
  • Every custody tells you how it ends. The cell used to be the only place that said what got you out and how long you had left. Cages, work camps and coffles all say it now — your ways out, and the days remaining.
  • Panel keys toggle. The key that opens the backpack closes the backpack, and the same goes for the rest of the panels. An open panel no longer swallows clicks meant for the thing behind it.
